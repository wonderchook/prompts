## Weekly Synthesis

### Key Themes This Week

1. **AI governance consulting demand is real but fragmented.** UNICEF, WHO, the World Bank, and UK government are all seeking AI governance expertise, but each through different procurement channels (UN consultancies, expert groups, government frameworks, grant RFPs). There is no single portal to watch — you must monitor each pipeline separately.

2. **Mozilla is the standout funder this cycle.** Two live opportunities (Human-Scale AI Incubator, Nov 20; Fellowship LOI, Dec 17) with strong alignment. Mozilla's framing — "trustworthy AI" and "human agency" — maps directly to the socio-technical leadership profile. Both are prestigious and well-compensated.

3. **The Humanity AI coalition signals a large incoming funding pool.** $500M committed, only $18M disbursed. This is the biggest philanthropic bet on public-interest AI since the original Hewlett/Ford digital governance investments. Future rounds will likely be larger and more frequent.

4. **World Bank's new digital strategy creates a procurement wave.** The October 2026 digital strategy launch — including an AI Infrastructure and Readiness Initiative — will generate substantial consulting demand over the next 12–18 months. Getting on the radar now, before specific solicitations post, is strategic.

5. **Coefficient Giving is between rounds.** The top AI governance funder is explicitly "likely to open more RFPs" but hasn't set a date. This is a prime candidate for proactive outreach — expressing interest and sharing a research agenda before the next round opens.

### Gaps Where Proactive Pitching May Work

- **Coefficient Giving / Open Philanthropy:** Pre-RFP relationship building. The funder is actively looking for AI governance projects and has indicated new rounds are coming.
- **World Bank Digital & AI VP:** The new vice presidency is staffing up. Cold outreach with a specific proposal for AI readiness assessment or digital public infrastructure advisory work could land well.
- **Humanity AI coalition members:** Individual member foundations (especially Ford, Omidyar, and MacArthur) may fund work outside the pooled fund. Direct approaches to their AI program officers with a specific project concept could yield results.
- **DPGA / Open Data Institute:** The DPG ecosystem is growing and needs senior advisory capacity on AI-as-DPG questions. The June 2026 UNU/ADB assessment found very few AI systems meet DPG standards — there's a consultancy waiting to be scoped here.

### Notable Signals

- **Frontier lab safety evaluation is professionalizing.** The Anthropic/OpenAI/DeepMind discussions on shared safety standards and third-party evaluators could create a new class of independent evaluation contracts. Worth monitoring.
- **OECD data:** Only 20% of OECD enterprises with 10+ employees used AI in 2025. The gap between policy ambition and actual adoption is a consulting opportunity in itself — particularly around AI readiness assessment and responsible deployment.
- **USAID uncertainty:** USAID's operational status has shifted significantly. Digital development consulting through USAID channels may be limited. Alternative pathways (World Bank, bilateral programs, foundation funding) are more reliable for now.
