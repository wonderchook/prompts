## Weekly Synthesis

### Key Themes This Week

1. **AI capability evaluation is becoming its own consulting category.** The IST advisor role is the clearest signal: frontier labs, evaluators, and policymakers need people who can design testable evaluation protocols for AI capabilities. This is no longer a research-only domain -- it's procurement-ready advisory work.

2. **Philanthropic AI funding has consolidated and scaled.** The Humanity AI coalition ($500M from ten foundations) is the defining development of 2026. It centralizes what was previously fragmented foundation giving into a single pooled mechanism. The upcoming $10M open call is the first test of whether this structure can move money to community-led organizations. Anyone doing public-interest AI work should be watching this closely.

3. **Digital public goods infrastructure is professionalizing.** UNICEF, DPGA, and the broader UN ecosystem are moving from pilot-stage DPG work to standing technical advisory and coordination roles. These are no longer experimental -- they're operational and hiring.

4. **Government AI governance RFPs are going global.** Malaysia's dual-track approach (AI Governance Bill + National AI Action Plan) is a template other countries are following. The EU AI Act enforcement date (August 2, 2026) is driving similar consultancy needs across Europe.

5. **The "state capacity for AI" theme is emerging.** Rockefeller's AI Readiness Project and the broader pattern of state-level AI governance positions (California's AI Science Advisor at $190-220K, Oregon's AI Policy and Safety Advisor) signal a growing market for consulting to governments on AI implementation.

### Notable Patterns

- **Repeat funders to watch:** Coefficient Giving (largest AI governance funder, uses lightweight EOI process), Mozilla Foundation (annual fellowship + incubator cycles), Rockefeller Foundation (invitation-only but actively building AI portfolio), UNICEF Innovation Fund (consistent open-source technical advisory needs).
- **Regulatory deadlines creating consulting demand:** EU AI Act high-risk obligations take full effect August 2, 2026. Colorado SB 24-205 enforcement began February 2026. Both are generating compliance consulting needs at scale.
- **Recently closed but worth tracking for next cycle:** GovAI Research Fellow (Aug 16), UKRI Advisory Group (Aug 7), Coefficient Giving AI Governance RFP (Jan 2026), Mozilla Fellowship and Democracy x AI (2026 cohorts).

### Gaps Where Proactive Pitching May Work

1. **Rockefeller AI Readiness Project** -- As the initiative scales from 20-30 to 250 communities, it will need senior technical advisors who can bridge AI capability and government capacity. Approach through Center for Civic Futures.

2. **World Bank WDR 2026 follow-on work** -- The World Development Report on AI for Development launched August 4, 2026. Country-level implementation guidance and technical assistance will follow. The World Bank's consulting pipeline typically lags flagship publications by 3-6 months.

3. **Humanity AI pre-positioning** -- The $10M open call criteria haven't been released. Building relationships with any of the ten participating foundations now could shape positioning for the call.

4. **Post-EU AI Act compliance consulting** -- The August 2 enforcement date creates immediate demand for deployer obligation compliance consulting. Organizations in the $25M-$500M operator range are actively issuing AI governance RFPs.

5. **National AI governance bill consulting** -- Malaysia's model is replicable. Countries drafting AI legislation need both comparative analysis expertise and technical advisory. Watch for similar RFPs from ASEAN, African Union, and Pacific Island states.
