## Weekly Synthesis

### Key Themes This Week

1. **Agent systems are the new frontier for governance and safety funding.** Schmidt Sciences, Foresight Institute, Amazon, the AI Safety Fund, and the International AI Safety Report 2026 all center agentic AI — planning, tool use, multi-agent coordination, and autonomous action — as the primary concern. Funders are actively seeking proposals on evaluation frameworks, oversight mechanisms, and agent identity verification.

2. **Philanthropic AI funding is consolidating and scaling.** The Humanity AI coalition ($500M from 10 foundations) represents a step-change in coordinated philanthropic investment. Coefficient Giving (fka Open Philanthropy) continues to grow its AI Governance program. These are not one-off grants — they're building sustained programs that will need senior advisors and implementers for years.

3. **Digital public infrastructure is a global priority.** The World Bank's Global DPI Program, DPGA's climate-action collection, UNDP's DPI governance frameworks, and OECD's digital government reports all converge on the same thesis: countries need trusted digital systems (ID, payments, data exchange) and the governance capacity to run them. AI is now layered on top as both an enabler and a risk.

4. **AI governance training and capacity building are in high demand.** UNICEF (South Asia), the Rockefeller/CCF AI Readiness Project, and Coefficient Giving's capacity-building grants all seek people who can translate AI governance principles into practical training, working groups, and institutional practice.

### Notable Shifts

- **Coefficient Giving is shifting from reactive to proactive grantmaking.** They are allocating more staff capacity toward identifying critical gaps themselves, rather than waiting for proposals. This means unsolicited conversations with their program team about under-addressed risks could be productive.
- **The $200M+ in AI safety funding announced in Q1 2026 alone** (per Granted AI analysis) represents a massive increase in available resources. Competition will be fierce, but the volume of funding means well-differentiated proposals have room.
- **UNICEF and UNDP are both scaling AI-related consulting** across multiple regions and program areas simultaneously. The UN system is clearly investing in building internal AI capacity, creating a sustained pipeline of consulting opportunities.

### Gaps — Where Proactive Pitching May Work

- **AI evaluation infrastructure for non-Western contexts.** Most evaluation and benchmarking work is US/EU-centric. A proposal to build evaluation capacity for AI systems deployed in South/Southeast Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa, or Latin America would fill a real gap — fundable by Coefficient Giving, Humanity AI member foundations, or UNDP.
- **Socio-technical risk assessment for agent deployments in public services.** Governments are deploying agentic AI (per the OECD report, 200+ government AI use cases) faster than they're assessing risks. A consulting practice or funded research program on agent risk assessment for government use cases has no strong incumbent.
- **Open data governance for AI training pipelines.** The convergence of AI governance and data governance (noted in the Cybersecurity Insiders May 2026 analysis) creates an opportunity for someone who understands both open data ecosystems and AI training data requirements to advise on policy frameworks.
- **Bridge between Humanity AI coalition foundations and technical implementers.** The coalition has $500M and is ramping grants — but the member foundations historically fund advocacy and policy, not engineering. Someone who can translate between engineering reality and philanthropic strategy is scarce and valuable.
