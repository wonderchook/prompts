## Weekly Synthesis

### Key Themes This Week

1. **Humanity AI is now deploying capital.** The $10M open call (September 10) marks the operational launch of the largest pooled philanthropic fund for public-interest AI. This is a structural shift — not just another grant. The coalition's ten foundations collectively represent a new funding center of gravity, and the first open call will set expectations for what "community-led AI" means in practice.

2. **Federal AI evaluation infrastructure is crystallizing.** The NIST TEVV-Athlon framework and the GSA-NIST partnership are building the standards and procurement scaffolding that will govern how the US government buys and deploys AI. This is a once-in-a-cycle window for shaping these frameworks while they're still in draft.

3. **The AI governance consulting market is splitting.** High-end strategic work (evaluation design, governance architecture, policy development) is separating from compliance-as-a-service (EU AI Act checkbox consulting, vendor risk assessments). The opportunities in Tier 1 and Tier 2 cluster on the strategic side — the compliance market is being absorbed by the Big Four and product vendors.

4. **Fellowship and research slots are thinning.** Several strong 2026 fellowships (Horizon AI for Science, CIP, GovAI Summer) have closed. The IAPS fellowship (deadline September 27) is one of the last high-quality slots for this cycle. The Civic Science Fellows program still appears to have open positions.

### Notable Shifts

- **Coefficient Giving's formal AI Governance RFP is closed**, but the Technical AI Safety RFP and the general Apply for Funding pathway remain open. The center of gravity has shifted from governance-policy toward technical safety research.
- **The UK is spending aggressively on AI** (£1.41B in 2026) but the market is dominated by incumbents. SME and independent consultants face structural barriers.
- **NIST is actively seeking external expertise** through the TEVV-Athlon comment process. This is unusual — NIST typically receives industry comments; independent expert input is more valued and rarer.

### Gaps Where Proactive Pitching May Work

1. **NIST/GSA AI evaluation consulting.** No active RFP, but the MOU is generating workstreams. A well-timed cold outreach to CAISI, paired with a substantive TEVV-Athlon comment, could position for future task orders.

2. **Patrick J. McGovern Foundation.** No open call, but they're actively deploying. The AI Trust Benchmark grant to Full Fact signals interest in evaluation infrastructure. A warm introduction with a concrete proposal concept around AI system evaluation for public benefit could land.

3. **Omidyar Network / Co-Develop Fund.** Active in digital public infrastructure and AI governance but no current open solicitation identified. Their sustained role in the DPGA and DPI ecosystem suggests rolling engagement opportunities.

4. **Digital Europe Programme beyond cybersecurity.** The ECCC call is cybersecurity-framed, but the broader Digital Europe Programme includes calls for AI regulatory compliance tools (DIGITAL-2026-AI-DATA-10-COMPLIANCE). Worth monitoring the Funding & Tenders portal for calls with better thematic fit.

### Repeat Funders / Organizations

- **Coefficient Giving** appears across three opportunities (Technical AI Safety, AI Governance, Global Health). They are the single largest funder in this space.
- **NIST** appears twice (TEVV-Athlon + GSA partnership). They are the US government's de facto AI evaluation standard-setter.
- **Humanity AI coalition members** (Ford, MacArthur, Omidyar, Mozilla) appear individually and collectively across multiple opportunities. The coalition's open call may subsume or redirect some individual foundation grantmaking.
