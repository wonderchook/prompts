## Weekly Synthesis

### Key Themes

1. **Philanthropy is the fastest-moving channel right now.** The Humanity AI open call and Cities Deliver represent over $60M in active funding for public-interest AI work. Both launched in the last 30 days. The pooled-fund model (ten foundations in Humanity AI, two in Cities Deliver) is becoming the dominant pattern for large-scale AI-for-good funding — expect more of these.

2. **City/government partnerships are becoming table stakes.** Both Cities Deliver and the Google.org challenge (closed) require a government partner. This signals that funders want demonstrated demand-side commitment, not just supply-side proposals. Practitioners with existing government relationships have a structural advantage.

3. **AI governance consulting is splitting into two markets.** Enterprise governance (ISO 42001, NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act compliance) is consolidating around Big 4 firms. Public-interest governance (community participation, socio-technical risk, development contexts) remains fragmented and open to independent practitioners. The latter is where the opportunities above sit.

4. **The UK is actively procuring AI with a domestic preference.** The £100M Sovereign AI scheme and the MOD fast lane are significant, but the UK-supplier preference creates barriers for non-UK-based consultants.

5. **Fellowship programs are cycling into Q1 2027.** Pivotal (Nov 1 deadline), OpenAI Safety (closed, next cohort TBD), and GovAI (closed) are all winter/spring programs. The pattern suggests a November–December window for Q1 2027 applications across multiple organizations.

### Notable Shifts

- **USAID dissolution complicates international development AI work.** USAID's functions moved under the State Department in 2025, and its digital development programs were heavily cut. The Digital Frontiers mechanism and related contract vehicles may no longer operate as they did. This shifts demand toward multilateral channels (UNDP, World Bank) and philanthropic funders.
- **Pooled philanthropic funds are new.** Humanity AI's coalition model didn't exist a year ago. It changes the landscape by concentrating funding decisions and creating a single point of entry for multiple foundation portfolios.
- **The DPGA's 2021–2026 strategy is concluding.** This likely means a new strategic plan and new consulting needs. The ODI's recent membership signals expanded scope.

### Gaps and Proactive Pitching Opportunities

- **AI evaluation and benchmarking consulting.** The Vals AI Fellowship (closed) and DoorDash's fellowship both focused on evaluation — but no consulting RFP for AI evaluation work appeared. This is a gap: organizations need evaluation frameworks but aren't yet procuring them through formal channels. Cold outreach to AI labs, foundations, and government AI offices with an evaluation governance pitch could land pre-RFP.
- **Agent systems governance.** Despite rapid agent deployment in enterprise and government, no specific opportunity for agent-systems governance consulting appeared. The OECD Digital Government Outlook explicitly names AI agents as a governance challenge. A short concept note to OECD, GovAI, or NIST could open a conversation.
- **Digital public goods + AI intersection.** The ADB/UNU report found very few AI systems meeting the DPG standard. This gap is a concrete consulting opportunity for anyone who can bridge AI deployment and DPG certification. The DPGA and ODI are natural partners.
- **Post-WDR 2026 advisory.** The World Bank's AI for Development report will create demand for country-level implementation advisory. Positioning now — before publication — is more effective than competing after.
