## Weekly Synthesis

### Key Themes This Week

1. **The Rockefeller/OpenAI Cities Deliver program is the week's headline.** A $50M fund with a November 29 deadline, focused on AI for city public services. The scale, the public-interest framing, and the "choose your own AI tools" latitude make this the most actionable opportunity in this scan. It requires an institutional partner (nonprofit or public-benefit corp), so the immediate action is identifying a teaming partner if one isn't already in place.

2. **The World Bank is hiring aggressively in digital and AI.** Three distinct roles surfaced: Trustworthy AI frameworks (strategic), internal AI governance (institutional), and AI automation (technical). The Bank's new Digital and AI Vice Presidency is building capacity, and the VP-level reorganization creates openings for experienced people who can bridge technical AI work and development-sector governance.

3. **Multi-agent AI safety is an emerging funding theme.** Schmidt Sciences' multi-agent safety RFP (closed but with no announced awards) and the Foresight Institute's Coordination and Accountability track both point toward growing funder interest in the risks of interacting AI systems. This is a space where socio-technical systems expertise is scarce and in demand.

4. **Major AI governance RFP rounds have cycled.** Coefficient Giving, Humanity AI ($10M open call), and Mozilla's Democracy x AI cohort all closed in the past few months. The pipeline is temporarily dry, but Coefficient Giving has signaled more RFPs are coming, and Humanity AI's first open call suggests future rounds.

5. **US federal AI procurement is active but chaotic.** The AFRL BAA remains open through 2028 and keeps expanding scope. The DHS procurement modernization CSO is in an unclear state. USAID's digital portfolio has contracted significantly. The pattern: DoD is spending, civilian agencies are reorganizing.

### Notable Shifts vs. Previous Weeks

- **Pooled philanthropic funds are operationalizing.** Humanity AI's $10M open call (closed October 21) was its first disbursement round from a $500M commitment. Expect more calls in 2027 as the fund builds its grantmaking cadence.
- **Coefficient Giving's org-incubation call** is a different model than the standard RFP cycle — they're looking for founders, not proposals. This signals a shift toward building new institutions rather than funding existing ones.
- **The World Bank's VP-level digital reorganization** is creating a burst of hiring that may not last. The window for joining the team that sets institutional AI governance norms is narrower than it looks.

### Gaps Where Proactive Pitching May Work

1. **UNDP's DAI Hub and Chief Digital Office** are actively building out digital public goods programming. A cold pitch offering senior advisory on DPG governance frameworks — especially one that bridges the DPGA registry work with country-level implementation — could land.

2. **UNESCO's comparative AI governance review** (the CULTURAI seven-jurisdiction study) is exactly the kind of work that recurs. Even if the August posting is closed, a note to the Ethics of AI unit flagging availability for future rounds is worth sending.

3. **Coefficient Giving's "new organizations" call** is explicitly looking for people with ideas. If there's a thesis about an AI governance gap that isn't being filled, this is the moment to pitch it — before the next formal RFP cycle begins.

4. **The AI agent evaluation space** is underfunded relative to its importance. A well-scoped proposal on evaluation frameworks for multi-agent systems could be pitched to Coefficient Giving, Schmidt Sciences, or the Cooperative AI Foundation as a pre-RFP conversation.
